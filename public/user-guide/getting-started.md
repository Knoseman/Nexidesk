# Getting Started with Nexidesk

Welcome to Nexidesk — a clean, shared workspace built for support teams that actually want to enjoy their tools. This guide will get you oriented so you can start crushing tickets right away.

---

## What is Nexidesk?

Nexidesk turns every email sent to your shared mailbox into a ticket. Your whole team sees every conversation, replies from the same address, and keeps track of what needs doing — without anything getting lost in personal inboxes.

It is intentionally simple. It does not try to do everything. It does one thing brilliantly: tracking conversations from the first email all the way to resolution.

---

## The Layout

Your screen is split into three panels:

| Panel | Position | What it shows |
|-------|----------|---------------|
| **Ticket List** | Left | Your queue — tickets filtered and sorted by recent activity |
| **Ticket Detail** | Centre | The full conversation, header controls, and reply box |
| **Right Rail** | Right | Requester details, company info, and tags |

**Pro tip:** If the list feels cramped, click the chevron at the top of the left panel to collapse it. The detail panel will stretch out and breathe.

## How Tickets Move Through the System

Every ticket has a status. This is how your team knows what still needs attention.

| Status | What it means |
|--------|---------------|
| **New** | The ticket has arrived but no one has looked at it yet. |
| **Open** | Someone is actively working on it. |
| **Pending** | You are waiting for the customer to reply, or for something outside the team. |
| **Resolved** | You believe the issue is sorted. The ticket stays visible in case the customer needs to come back. |
| **Closed** | Finished. A new reply from the customer automatically reopens it. |

### Good habits

- Use **Pending** when you are waiting on the customer. Do not leave tickets as Open if you are not actively working on them — your team needs an honest view of the workload.
- Use **Resolved** as a deliberate step. It means "I think this is done", not "I have just replied".
- You do not need to babysit **Closed** tickets. If the customer writes back, the ticket reopens to Open automatically. Magic.

### Priority levels

| Priority | When to use it |
|----------|----------------|
| **Low** | No rush — can be dealt with in a batch. |
| **Normal** | The default. Standard response times apply. |
| **High** | Something is broken or the customer has escalated. |
| **Urgent** | Serious outage, data at risk, or a breached contract. |

Priority is a signal to your team, not the customer. They cannot see it. Be real — if everything is Urgent, the label loses its power.
